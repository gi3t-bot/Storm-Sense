FLOOD PREPARATION



Floods

Failing to evacuate flooded areas, entering flood waters, or remaining after a flood has passed can result in injury or death. Flooding is a temporary overflow of water onto land that is normally dry. Floods may:
  • Result from rain, snow, coastal storms, storm surges, and dam failures.
  • Develop slowly or rapidly, flash floods may occur without warning.
  • Cause outages, disrupt transportation, and damage buildings.

IF YOU ARE UNDER A FLOOD WARNING, FIND SAFE SHELTER RIGHT AWAY

  • Do not walk, swim, or drive through flood waters.
  • Six inches of water can knock you down.
  • One foot of moving water can sweep a vehicle away.
  • Stay off bridges over fast-moving water.
  • Determine the safest action for your situation.
  • Evacuate if told to do so.
  • Move to higher ground.

Prepare NOW

  • Know flood risks in your area.
  • Sign up for emergency alerts.
  • Monitor heavy rainfall.
  • Practice evacuation routes.
  • Prepare emergency supplies.
  • Purchase flood insurance.
  • Protect important documents.
  • Protect your property.

SURVIVE DURING

  • Go to your identified safe location.
  • Evacuate immediately if ordered.
  • Follow official alerts.
  • Turn Around, Don’t Drown!
  • Stay off bridges.
  • If trapped in a vehicle, stay inside.
  • If trapped in a building, go to the highest level.

Be Safe AFTER

  • Return home only when authorities say it is safe.
  • Avoid driving unless necessary.
  • Beware of wildlife and debris.
  • Avoid electrical hazards.
  • Avoid contaminated floodwater.
  • Use generators outdoors only.
